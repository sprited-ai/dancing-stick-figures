\begin{abstract}
Video-generation research has a steep entry cost: real video is large, generating factors are hidden, and a single
distributional score does not explain temporal failure. We introduce \textbf{Dancing Stick Figures}, a fully traced
synthetic testbed that packages generation, training, long-horizon sampling, and diagnosis for consumer GPUs and
hosted notebooks. The release contains 1,430 six-second motions rendered from three cameras (4,290 videos, 514,800
frames) with joints, camera state, depth, normals, segmentation, and raw motion. We freeze a primary $64^2$, 50-frame,
10-fps protocol whose two real-reference sets contain 128 source-motion-disjoint videos each. Controlled baselines
expose complementary metric behaviour: freezing collapses all one-sided motion errors to zero, while reversing every
video changes FVD only from 119.0 to 129.7; shuffling, freezing, and looping increase it to 335.7--482.4. Acceleration
and angular jerk detect shuffling and looping but, by construction, not reversal. These results motivate reporting
interpretable motion signals beside FVD rather than treating either as a complete quality score. Released image,
short-video, autoregressive, and text-conditioned models make the testbed runnable. In a matched 10k-step,
50-frame text-to-video comparison ($n=64$), image warm-starting reduces topology violations from .406 to .107 and
increases prompt-to-noise sensitivity from .360 to .550, but moves centroid speed farther from the real reference.
The result is a measured tradeoff, not a claim of uniform improvement or total-compute efficiency.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Small video experiments often omit one of three things: controllable data, a runnable baseline, or an evaluator that
names the failure. Dancing Stick Figures integrates these pieces into one small, modifiable loop. Our goal is not to
approximate photorealistic video, but to make data generation, model training, and structural diagnosis inspectable on
hardware available to students and small laboratories.

The release contributes four implemented pieces:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a label-rich video dataset with a 0.85-GB $64^2$ configuration and regenerable rendering state;
\item a frozen, source-motion-disjoint 50-frame protocol and controlled failure suite;
\item a Colab route through image training, video warm-starting, rollout, and scoring; and
\item reference image, short-video, autoregressive, and text-conditioned 50-frame diffusion approaches whose outputs can be diagnosed with
topology and motion signals alongside FVD.
\end{enumerate}
We additionally publish multiple baseline families. A hosted leaderboard, conditional text benchmark, and learned
geometry evaluator are \emph{not} part of v0.1; Section~\ref{sec:future} lists them as work rather than claims.

\section{Related Work}
Moving MNIST~\cite{srivastava2015unsupervised} is runnable but has little articulated structure. Kubric/MOVi
~\cite{greff2022kubric} provides rich synthetic supervision for general scene understanding. AMASS and
HumanML3D~\cite{mahmood2019amass,guo2022humanml3d} expose human motion rather than a complete pixel-video learning
loop. Our testbed instead joins articulated motion, deterministic rendering, small generative baselines, and a
structure-specific diagnostic.

Modern video generators span full-context and causal designs at a scale far beyond this testbed. Wan combines a video
VAE with diffusion transformers in an open model family~\cite{wan2025}; Seedance 1.0 jointly supports T2I, T2V, and
I2V and uses image--video training stages~\cite{gao2025seedance}. MAGI-1 instead predicts video chunks
autoregressively~\cite{magi2025}. Diffusion Forcing assigns separate noise levels across a causal sequence, while Self
Forcing trains autoregressive video diffusion on its own generated context to reduce train--test exposure
bias~\cite{chen2024diffusionforcing,huang2025selfforcing}. These systems motivate our architecture ladder, but our
40M-parameter pixel models are controlled teaching baselines rather than scaled competitors.

Table~\ref{tab:modern} separates several ideas that are easily conflated under the label ``video DiT.'' Modern
systems usually compress video before denoising; they then choose full-context, factorised, or causal attention. Our
K1 deliberately removes the learned tokenizer. This is not an efficiency claim: it lets a learner inspect the direct
mapping between RGBA pixels, patches, attention axes, and the structure-specific evaluator.

Evaluation has likewise moved beyond a single distributional score. T2V-CompBench tests compositional prompt binding
with MLLM-, detection-, and tracking-based measurements~\cite{sun2025compbench}; VBench-2.0 targets human fidelity,
controllability, physics, and commonsense~\cite{zheng2025vbench2}. Our colour-topology oracle is deliberately narrower:
it gives cheap, interpretable diagnostics for one traced domain and is not a replacement for semantic or human-aligned
evaluation.

We call v0.1 a \emph{testbed}, not a mature architecture benchmark. Benchmark rankings can be fragile to task and
metric choice~\cite{dehghani2021lottery}; our UNet and DiT runs also differ in parameter count and training objective.
They are reference approaches, not evidence that one architecture is generally superior. A valid architecture track
requires compute-matched recipes, repeated seeds, and tests across renderer shifts.

\section{Dataset and research harness}
NVIDIA ARDY~\cite{zhao2026ardy} generates ten motions for each of 143 prompts in dance, gesture, locomotion,
transition, idle, and sport groups. Each motion is six seconds at 20 fps. A z-buffered capsule renderer with
$4\times$ supersampling emits three deterministic orthographic views. The public data are summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:data}. Prompts, rather than seeds or cameras, define the split; all views of one prompt stay together.
The 37-prompt test set includes all 33 sport prompts as an unseen semantic group.

The harness samples limb length, scale, stroke, camera yaw and pitch deterministically from \texttt{clip\_id}. It
stores 3D and projected joints, visibility, camera and body parameters, RGBA, 16-bit depth, camera-space normals,
27-part segmentation, and raw motion. This makes renderer changes experimentally useful: a researcher can vary visual
domain or camera policy and still measure against exact latent state. The current release has one style, one figure per
frame, orthographic cameras, and $128^2$ maximum resolution.

\section{Tasks and failure diagnostics}
\paragraph{Primary task.} Every video approach submits $64^2\!\times\!50$ frames representing five seconds at 10
fps. Autoregressive models use eight context and eight new frames internally; six chunks produce 56 frames and the
protocol scores the first 50. For short-video and image models, 8-frame windows and individual frames are local
training primitives rather than different evaluation tasks. The text-conditioned K1 experiment uses the
prompt-disjoint split, but its prompt-swap measurement establishes sensitivity rather than semantic correctness;
a scored prompt-adherence track remains TODO.

\paragraph{Structural oracle.} Each limb chain has a fixed proximal/distal colour pair. Nearest-palette parsing turns
an RGBA frame into masks, after which the evaluator reports: TVR, the fraction of eight limb colours whose visible mask
does not have exactly one connected component; LIE, the fraction of eight expected colour adjacencies that are missing;
CPE, off-palette foreground; and clean rate, where TVR and LIE are zero. Video evaluation adds colour-mass drift,
centroid speed and acceleration, the fraction of moving transitions, angular speed and jerk, and
height variation. Motion amount is a two-sided signal: zero speed indicates collapse rather than perfection. These are
image-space diagnostics, not biomechanics.

Occlusion produces non-zero topology errors even on ground-truth renderings. We therefore call ground-truth scores a
\emph{real reference}, not an error floor. A deterministic manifest selects one camera per source motion and holds the
two 128-video references motion-disjoint. FVD~\cite{unterthiner2018fvd} uses the same native 50-frame videos and sample
count; no frames are repeated to satisfy its temporal input.

\paragraph{Controlled validation.} We rerendered 500 held-out frames and applied five deterministic structural
failures. Partial left/right colour swap increases LIE by .203 and an extra arm increases TVR by .085. Full-chain swap,
bone stretch, and hand deletion expose blind spots because v0 checks visible colour topology rather than proportions,
end-effectors, or semantic left/right identity.

\section{Reference approaches and results}
The image suite includes a 46M-parameter factorised UNet trained with cosine diffusion and v-prediction, and a
33M-parameter factorised DiT trained with rectified flow~\cite{ho2020ddpm,peebles2023dit,lipman2023flow}. At $64^2$,
released checkpoints cover 30k--100k steps; $128^2$ variants are also released. They demonstrate runnable model
families rather than an architecture ranking because objectives, parameter counts, and compute differ.

Short-video UNets test random initialization against initialization from the 100k image model. Both use the same
factorised architecture and 8-frame training window. An earlier exploratory comparison sampled random validation
windows and reused source clips across real FVD halves; we withdraw those numeric claims rather than relabel them as
final evidence. Corrected validation now fixes windows, timesteps, and noise. The autoregressive UNet conditions each
new 8-frame chunk on eight prior frames and is the primary released five-second model. Its multi-seed 50-frame table,
including error by distance from chunk boundaries, remains TODO.

\paragraph{Metric stress test.} Figure~\ref{fig:failures} provides the first result under the corrected protocol.
The two independent real sets have centroid speeds .498 and .500 pixels per frame. Freezing makes all motion signals
zero; frame shuffling raises centroid acceleration from .564 to 3.120 and angular jerk from .094 to .347; looping the
first eight frames raises them to 1.298 and .177. Reversal preserves the frame set and time-symmetric motion signals.
FVD is 119.0 for real--real, 379.5 for freezing, 335.7 for shuffling, 482.4 for looping, and 129.7 for reversal. In 30
paired 64-video subset trials, reversal increased FVD in every trial, but by only $9.3\pm6.8$ versus real--real. Thus
FVD detects reversal weakly in this setting while the interpretable signals are explicitly time-symmetric; neither
measurement alone certifies directionally correct motion.

\paragraph{Matched K1 warm-start experiment.} K1 is a 39.9M-parameter pixel-space DiT with alternating spatial and
temporal attention, frozen T5-small text features, and a rectified-flow objective. We trained a random-initialized
control and an otherwise matched treatment initialized from a 30k-step, single-frame text-to-image checkpoint. Both
video runs use the same $64^2\!\times\!50$ data, split, optimizer, seed, and 10k-step budget, mixing 70\% T2V, 20\%
I2V, and 10\% T2I batches. Evaluation uses 64 held-out prompts with paired noise, 50 Euler steps, CFG 3, and a
hash-gated copy of the released colour dataset.

Image warm-starting lowers final validation loss from .0159 to .0118 and TVR from .406 to .107, a 73.5\% reduction
(paired 95\% CI for the absolute change $[-.336,-.261]$). Foreground occupancy moves toward the real reference
(.102 to .076 versus .060), and angular jerk falls from .335 to .285. The prompt-swap/noise-swap distance ratio rises
from .360 to .550, indicating stronger prompt sensitivity, not verified prompt correctness. The gain is not uniform:
centroid speed rises from .571 to .674 versus a .416 real reference, and centroid acceleration and motion fraction
also move farther away. Figure~\ref{fig:k1} makes this tradeoff explicit. Because image pretraining costs an additional
30k steps, the experiment does not establish total-compute efficiency. It is also a single-seed result, and the warm
run resumed from step 6000 after a storage interruption; model, EMA, and optimizer state were restored, but random
number and data-order state restarted.

The ladder is intentionally cumulative rather than a leaderboard. M0 validates the instruments; M1 isolates spatial
learning; M2 introduces the full temporal task; and M3 changes only initialization. Students can therefore connect a
visible failure to the additional machinery introduced at the next stage without mistaking model scale for insight.

\paragraph{Why full-clip bidirectionality stops being enough.} Bidirectional temporal attention is useful inside
K1's fixed 50-frame sample because every frame can coordinate with both earlier and later frames. The limitation
appears first in training cost. Every update must materialize and backpropagate through all 50 noisy output frames;
increasing the trained horizon grows activation memory across the network and the temporal-attention work
quadratically. In our uncompressed pixel setting, a longer horizon therefore quickly reduces batch size and makes each
optimization step substantially slower. The same interface also does not support native sliding-window continuation:
K1 jointly noises and predicts the entire window, rather than freezing an overlapping clean prefix and applying noise
and loss only to newly generated frames. For motion-dominated video, the latter is also an easier teacher-forced
learning problem: clean history already constrains identity, position, pose, velocity, and action phase, leaving the
model to denoise a smaller and less ambiguous future block rather than invent the full trajectory jointly. This
training advantage does not guarantee easier rollout, because generated history introduces exposure bias and error
accumulation. Independently sampled windows can reset pose or action phase, whereas treating overlap as ordinary noisy
output can rewrite already played history. A single prompt conditions the entire 50-frame
sample, so it also cannot be changed during playback while preserving the completed prefix and regenerating only the
remaining future; the system must wait for the next clip or discard and regenerate the joint sample. Real-time serving
is consequently awkward: playback cannot begin until the joint sample is sufficiently denoised, generation cannot be
naturally pipelined behind playback in bounded blocks, and a prompt update invalidates the prefetched future. Finally,
fixed 50-frame evaluation cannot reveal drift, repetition, or recovery after later continuation boundaries. This is a
task mismatch, not evidence that causal attention is universally superior. Our long-horizon track will instead test a
variable-history block-autoregressive model: completed history is immutable, the prompt may change at each new block,
and frames inside that block still interact bidirectionally, following the windowed distinction used by interactive
motion and chunked-video systems~\cite{zhao2026ardy,magi2025}.

\section{Accessibility and reproducibility}
The repository releases the generator, cache builder, trainers, rollout code, oracle, controlled corruptions, FVD
evaluation, checkpoints, and comparison script. The notebook inspects \texttt{mini}, builds a cache, trains an image
model, initializes a video model, renders a GIF, and compares oracle scores. Its cells have been verified stepwise on a
T4 and contain real outputs; a fresh uninterrupted run after the latest mixed-precision and batch-size fixes remains
TODO. This qualification matters for an educational testbed: a notebook should not be called reproducible merely
because its code is visible.

Code is MIT and released data are CC0-1.0. Motion was generated with ARDY under the NVIDIA Open Model License; users
must independently determine whether the source-model terms fit their use.

\section{Limitations and research tracks}\label{sec:future}
{\small
The current testbed is deliberately small, but its next tracks must be measurable:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Geometry track (TODO):} train a released 2D/3D pose regressor from exact labels; validate it on malformed
renders; add bone-length, joint-angle, and left/right identity errors.
\item \textbf{Conditional track (TODO):} validate prompt correctness on held-out prompts and the unseen sport group
with retrieval, motion semantics, or blinded human judgments; the current prompt-swap result measures sensitivity only.
\item \textbf{Architecture benchmark (TODO):} compare UNet, DiT, and later models under matched data, parameter or
compute budgets, sampling cost, repeated seeds, and confidence intervals; test whether rankings survive renderer shifts.
\item \textbf{Long-horizon track (TODO):} report error by distance from chunk boundaries and compare autoregression,
sliding-window diffusion, and one-shot longer-context models.
\item \textbf{Renderer tracks (TODO):} publish fixed palette, camera, and appearance shifts produced by the harness;
test whether methods improve structure or only fit one raster style.
\item \textbf{Course leaderboard (TODO):} freeze an evaluation container and hidden test split so student and lab
submissions can improve on public baselines without changing the metric implementation.
\end{enumerate}
}

\section{Conclusion}
Dancing Stick Figures lowers the cost of entering video generation while keeping the data-generating state observable.
The controlled results show that no reported metric is sufficient alone: one-sided smoothness rewards freezing, while
FVD responds far less to reversal than to freezing, shuffling, or looping. A traced, modifiable dataset makes these
responses observable and reproducible. The matched K1 experiment likewise shows why diagnosis matters: image
pretraining greatly improves colour topology while worsening several translation statistics. The release is therefore
a research testbed with a frozen starter protocol; calling it a
mature architecture benchmark remains contingent on corrected multi-seed model comparisons and evaluator validation.
